    Triple A Gas — Website Style Guide

    A design system for a dual-purpose site: a public storefront for customers across Ghana, and an internal operations tool for the Triple A Gas team — built from a study of how LPG and fuel marketing companies across Ghana and West Africa actually build their websites.

    What this document is

    A page-by-page reference covering research findings, brand foundations, color, type, components, page templates for the public site, and a separate design language for the internal admin/logs portal. Treat it the way a design or development team would treat a client style guide — pull tokens and rules directly from it when building.

    Who it's for

    • Whoever builds the public-facing Triple A Gas site
    • Whoever builds the internal logs/notes admin tool
    • Station managers and staff who will use the admin day-to-day
    • Anyone commissioning a logo, signage, or print material later

    The dual-purpose concept

    Every LPG company site researched for this guide (GOIL, GASO Petroleum, Trinity Oil, XpressGas in Ghana; Techno Oil in Nigeria) is public-only — a storefront with no visible internal tooling. Triple A Gas is different by design: one codebase, two experiences, split cleanly at the login wall.

    These two halves intentionally look different — the public site is warm, promotional, and photo-led; the admin portal is dark, dense, and data-led — while sharing the same color, type, and mark vocabulary so the whole product still feels like one company. Pages 10–11 cover the admin portal in full; pages 8–9 cover the public site.

    This guide does not include a final logo file. Page 3 defines a simple, buildable mark (the "gauge mark") that a designer can refine, and rules for how it should behave — enough to build the site now without blocking on a full brand identity project.

    Competitive Research Findings

    Patterns observed across GOIL, GASO Petroleum, Trinity Oil Co. Ltd, and XpressGas (Ghana), plus Techno Oil (Nigeria), as of July 2026. These are the conventions Ghanaian customers already expect from an LPG company's website — Triple A Gas should meet them, then differentiate through the gauge-mark identity and the admin portal.

    1. Navigation is need-based, not feature-based

    Every site studied organizes its main navigation around who the visitor is and what they came to do, not around internal company structure. GOIL splits its entire header into Individual vs Corporate visitors before anything else. GASO groups pages under plain-language headers like Who We Are, What We Do, and Sustainability rather than jargon. A persistent, high-contrast Find a Station / Station Locator link appears in the header on every site that has more than one location.

    2. Trust signals are front and center, not buried

    • Licensing & certification badges — GOIL displays ISO 9001 / ISO 14001 marks in the header on every page. XpressGas's own materials lead with its National Petroleum Authority license.
    • Named leadership — Trinity Oil lists its directors by name on the About page; GOIL has a full Board of Directors and Executive Management section.
    • Scale numbers — GASO runs a literal "GASO in Numbers" stat strip (litres delivered, employee count, station count, years of experience) directly on the homepage. This is the single most consistent homepage device across every site studied.
    • Safety as a named pillar, not a footnote — GOIL has a dedicated HSSEQ section (profile, policy, vision) and an LPG Safety Tips page; GASO's "Why Choose Us" tab panel opens on Safety before price or product.

    3. The station/outlet list is core content, not an afterthought

    GOIL publishes a full sortable table of every LPG filling plant it works with, by location. Trinity Oil lists every outlet by town (Accra, Kumasi, Sunyani, Asikuma, Kade, Koforidua, Somanya) with a direct phone number per outlet. This is treated as primary content, often ranking as one of the top navigation items, not tucked into a general contact page.

    4. Contact is phone-first and WhatsApp-first

    Phone numbers are large, tap-to-call, and repeated in the header, the footer, and a dedicated outlets/contact section on every site. GOIL surfaces a direct WhatsApp link in its header alongside its toll-free numbers. Formal contact forms exist but are secondary to a phone number a customer can call immediately — appropriate for a market where many customers order gas by phone rather than by web form.

    5. Visual identity leans corporate-industrial, not playful

    Across the set, primary palettes are dominated by a deep corporate blue or red (GOIL red, GASO's photography-led blue/white, Trinity's blue) with imagery of trucks, cylinders, filling plants, and staff in branded workwear. None of the sites studied use a flame or bright warning-orange as a primary brand color — orange/amber shows up only as a hazard or safety-signage accent, which is the gap this guide uses deliberately for Triple A Gas's flame accent (page 4).

    6. Product presentation is practical, not lifestyle marketing

    Cylinder sizes, refill vs. exchange, and delivery vs. pickup are explained plainly, often as a short service list rather than styled product cards: "LPG (Cooking Gas)," "Door to Door Delivery," "Distribution & Transportation" is a fairly literal example of how Trinity Oil frames its service list. Triple A Gas can raise the visual craft here (page 9) without abandoning the plain, task-first language customers are used to.

    7. Footers are consistent utility blocks

    Every footer studied repeats: physical head-office address, phone, email, social icons (Facebook is universal; LinkedIn and Instagram common; X/Twitter present but declining in emphasis), and a copyright line. Several sites (GOIL, GASO) also credit their web developer in the footer — a small local-industry convention worth keeping for Triple A Gas.

    Net takeaway for Triple A Gas: don't reinvent the public IA — a customer arriving from Google should immediately recognize "this is an LPG company site." Spend the design budget on craft (type, color, the gauge mark, photography direction) and on the admin portal, which none of these competitors have publicly at all.

    Brand Foundations

    What Triple A Gas should feel like before any pixel is placed.

    Positioning

    Triple A Gas is a dependable, safety-first LPG supplier serving households and businesses in Ghana. The "AAA" name should read the way it does in other industries — a mark of top-tier reliability — reinforced visually, not just claimed in copy.

    Three brand attributes

    Trustworthy

    Steady, plain-spoken, never oversells. Communicated through the deep Cylinder Blue, generous whitespace, and named/verifiable facts (license numbers, real outlet addresses, real phone lines).

    Safety-literate

    Safety is treated as a craft the company is proud of, not a legal disclaimer. Communicated through the Hazard Amber accent, the gauge motif, and a dedicated Safety section with real content, not boilerplate.

    Energetic

    The flame accent and Archivo Expanded display type give the brand more warmth and momentum than its more corporate-blue competitors, without tipping into "consumer lifestyle brand."

    The brand mark

    The Triple A Gas logo consists of three stylized letter 'A's that merge to form the shape of a clean blue LPG gas flame, styled in an elegant blue and orange/amber color palette. The mark represents safe, clean, and premium energy:

    Primary mark

    Three letter 'A' peaks, forming a unified corporate flame crest in clean cyan/blue gradients with a warm orange/red core. Perfect transparency allows it to blend on any background.

    Clear space & usage

    Keep clear space around the flame logo on all sides. Used in header navigations, app icons, and branding elements across the public site and admin portal.

    Set in Archivo Expanded 800, all caps, tracked open slightly. This lockup uses the approved premium vector flame logo featuring a stylized triple-A flame silhouette.

    Usage rules

    • Flame is a CTA and accent color, never a large background field — it should stay energetic, not overwhelming.
    • Hazard Amber is reserved for genuine safety/attention content. If it starts appearing on marketing banners "because it pops," that's a misuse — swap in Flame instead.
    • Cylinder Blue carries the brand's trust signal — it should be the dominant color of the header and footer on every public page.
    • On the admin side, color communicates status first, brand second. A red badge always means "needs attention," everywhere in the portal, with no exceptions.
    • Text on Cylinder Blue or Ink must be white or Cloud — verified for WCAG AA contrast (4.7:1 and 15.1:1 respectively).

    Typography

    Three families, each with one job. All three are free, widely mirrored fonts that render reliably on the mid-range Android devices most customers in Ghana will use.

    Aa

    Archivo Expanded — display. Headlines, page titles, stat numbers, the wordmark. Weights 700–900 only. Never for body copy or long paragraphs.

    Aa

    Inter — body & UI. Paragraphs, navigation, buttons, form fields, admin UI chrome. Weights 400 (body), 600 (labels/nav), 700–800 (emphasis).

    Aa

    IBM Plex Mono — data. Cylinder serial numbers, timestamps, prices, log entries, phone numbers in tables. Anywhere numeric alignment matters.

    Iconography, Photography & the Gauge Motif

    How Triple A Gas should look in photos and small graphics, and how the gauge mark extends beyond the logo into a full data-visualization device.

    Photography direction

    Do

    • Real cylinders, real trucks, real staff in branded workwear at real outlets
    • Natural daylight, slightly warm white balance
    • Wide shots of the filling plant/forecourt that show scale and order
    • Close-ups of the safety valve, gauge, and regulator to reinforce the safety story

    Avoid

    • Generic stock photography of unrelated gas burners or Western kitchens
    • Staged "lifestyle" smiling-at-camera shots with no product visible
    • Heavy color-graded filters that shift the blue/orange brand colors

    Icon style

    Line icons, 1.75px stroke, rounded caps, 24px grid — matching the rounded geometry of the gauge mark. Fill icons only for status badges (safe/hazard/danger).

    The gauge as a data device

    Beyond the logo, the gauge becomes a radial progress indicator anywhere the site needs to show a level, percentage, or completion state — tank stock level, safety-inspection compliance, delivery-route progress. This is the signature element that ties the public site's stat blocks to the admin portal's dashboards.

    UI Components

    Shared building blocks used across both the public site and the admin portal.

    Buttons

    Primary (Flame) is reserved for the single most important action on a page — usually "Order," "Call," or "WhatsApp Us." A page should rarely have more than one primary button visible at once.

    Badges

    In Stock Inspection Due Overdue Licensed Outlet

    Cards

    Outlet Card

    Name, town, phone (tap-to-call), open hours, distance from user if geolocation available.

    Product Card

    Cylinder size (kg), refill price, exchange vs. new-cylinder note, "Order" CTA.

    Log Entry Card

    Admin only. Timestamp, staff name, entry type badge, note text, attached photo if any.

    Forms

    Inputs: 1.5px Steel-300 border, 8px radius, Flame-colored focus ring, 44px minimum tap height. Every public form field should work with Ghanaian phone number formats and be usable one-thumbed.

    Admin Portal — Concept & Information Architecture

    The operational half of the site: where staff log deliveries, track cylinder inventory, record safety inspections, and leave shift notes. None of the competitor sites researched expose anything like this publicly — this is Triple A Gas's structural differentiator, so it should be genuinely useful, not a token "admin panel."

    Design intent

    The admin portal is a tool, not a marketing surface. It should feel calm and dense: dark UI to reduce glare in a station office, generous use of the mono type for scannable data, and status color used consistently and literally (page 4). It shares the gauge mark and status palette with the public site so the whole product still feels like one brand — but nothing about it should look "salesy."

    Core modules

    ModulePurposeWho uses it
    Delivery LogRecord each delivery: driver, route, cylinders/kg delivered, customer, timestamp, signature/photo proofDrivers, dispatch staff
    Cylinder InventoryTrack cylinder stock by size and status (full / empty / in transit / awaiting inspection) per outletStation managers
    Safety InspectionsScheduled and ad-hoc inspection records per cylinder or tank, pass/fail, photos, next-due dateSafety officer, station managers
    Shift NotesFree-text handover notes between shifts — issues, low stock warnings, customer complaints, maintenance needsAll station staff
    Staff DirectoryRoles, outlet assignment, contact info, permission levelAdmin/owner
    ReportsRolled-up view across outlets — stock levels, delivery volumes, overdue inspectionsOwner/head office

    Permission model

    Driver

    Create delivery log entries and shift notes. Read-only on inventory.

    Station Manager

    Full access to their own outlet's modules. Cannot see other outlets' financials.

    Owner / Head Office

    Full access across all outlets, plus Reports and Staff Directory.

    Data flow between the two halves

    Delivery Log entry
    →
    Inventory auto-updates
    →
    Station Locator "in stock" badge

    This is the real payoff of building both halves together: when a driver logs a delivery in the admin portal, the public "Find a Station" page can reflect live stock status instead of a static list — something none of the competitor sites offer.

    Keep the admin portal at a subdomain or subpath behind authentication (e.g. /portal), never linked from primary public navigation. A small, low-contrast "Staff Login" link in the footer is enough — see page 8.

    Admin-specific rules

    • Every log entry is timestamped and attributed automatically — never editable after save, only appendable (an "amended" follow-up entry), to preserve an honest audit trail.
    • Status badges use the exact same colors as the public site's status palette — the vocabulary must stay identical across both halves.
    • Tables default to newest-first. Staff scanning a handover need the latest entry without scrolling.
    • Design for a shared station tablet or desktop, not a personal phone — targets can be smaller (32–36px) than the public site's 44px minimum.
    • Works offline-first where possible: a shift note or delivery log drafted with no signal should queue and sync when connectivity returns, given inconsistent data coverage at some outlets.

    Voice, Tone & Bilingual Content

    How Triple A Gas sounds — in customer-facing copy and in the admin portal.

    Public voice

    • Plain, direct, confident — closer to Trinity Oil's practical service descriptions than to lifestyle-brand marketing copy.
    • Lead with what the customer can do: "Order a refill," "Find your nearest station" — active verbs, not passive claims.
    • Safety language is specific and instructional, never vague reassurance ("we care about safety") — give the actual step.
    • English is the primary language; short Twi/Ga phrases (e.g. a WhatsApp greeting, a safety-tip headline) can appear where they add warmth, but core instructions and legal/safety text stay in clear English so they're unambiguous.

    Example: homepage hero

    "Reliable cooking gas, delivered safely, across Ghana."

    Not: "Experience the future of energy with Triple A Gas's world-class solutions." — too abstract, doesn't say what the customer gets.

    Admin voice

    • Instructional and neutral. A field label says what to enter, not how to feel about it.
    • Error and empty states explain what happened and what to do next: "No inspections logged yet — add the first one" rather than a bare "No data."
    • Never use exclamation points or celebratory copy in the admin UI — this is a work tool used every day, and enthusiasm reads as noise.

    Accessibility, Responsive & Low-Bandwidth Rules

    Ghana's mobile web is overwhelmingly Android, often on 3G/4G with variable coverage outside major cities — every rule below is a direct response to that.

    Performance budget

    • Public homepage target: under 1.2MB total, under 3s to interactive on a mid-range Android over 4G.
    • Serve responsive, compressed images (WebP with JPEG fallback); never a full-resolution hero photo to a phone.
    • Fonts self-hosted or aggressively cached — don't block first paint on a webfont round-trip.
    • Admin portal: queue writes locally and sync in the background so a dropped connection at an outlet never loses a log entry (page 11).

    Responsive breakpoints

    BreakpointTargetNotes
    < 480pxSmall phonesSingle column everywhere, nav collapses to a menu, tap targets ≥44px
    480–900pxLarge phones / small tabletsPublic cards may go 2-up; admin stays single-column by default
    900–1200pxTablets / small laptopsAdmin sidebar becomes visible persistently; public grids go 3-up
    > 1200pxDesktopFull layouts as shown on pages 9 and 11

    Accessibility

    • All text/background pairs meet WCAG AA contrast — see the ratios noted on page 4.
    • Every interactive element has a visible keyboard focus ring in Flame.
    • Status is never color-only — badges always carry a text label alongside the color (page 7), so the site works for colorblind users and reads correctly in screen readers.
    • Forms have real <label> elements, not placeholder-only fields.
    • Respect prefers-reduced-motion — the gauge fill animation and any page transitions should become instant, not looping, when requested.

    Do's, Don'ts & Build Checklist

    A final pass before development starts.

    Do

    • Lead the public homepage with a stat bar and a clear, single CTA
    • Keep phone and WhatsApp contact visible on every public page
    • Treat the outlet/station list as primary content, fully searchable
    • Give Safety its own real page with actionable content
    • Keep the admin portal dark, dense, and status-color-driven
    • Make every admin log entry timestamped, attributed, and permanent
    • Reuse the gauge motif and status palette across both halves

    Don't

    • Bury the phone number behind a "Contact Us" click — it should always be one tap away
    • Use Hazard Amber decoratively outside of safety/status contexts
    • Link the admin portal from primary public navigation
    • Let staff edit or delete past log entries — amend only
    • Ship a hero image over ~300KB to mobile
    • Invent generic "world-class energy solutions" marketing copy — stay specific and plain (page 12)
